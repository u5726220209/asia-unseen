**The K-ETA is the most avoidable fee in Asia.** It is waived for several nationalities, it is not free to buy anyway, and the money does not come back if the application is refused.

Three facts settle it, and they are all on the government portal.

## The fee, and the word that matters

**10 000 won**, and **non-refundable** — including if the application is refused.

That second word is the one people discover late. A refusal is exactly the case in which a refund feels owed, and it is exactly the case in which there is none. The fee buys the processing, not the outcome.

## The waiver, and why this page does not list it

The requirement is **waived for several nationalities** under a scheme with a published end date. For those passports the K-ETA is optional rather than compulsory.

We are not reproducing the list here, and that is deliberate. It is a list that changes by ministerial notice, and a list copied into an article is a list that goes stale silently — the page still looks authoritative while the facts have moved underneath it. **The list lives on the portal, and the portal is the only version that is current.**

What we do hold, with its source and the month it was read, is the rule for your own passport: the [South Korea page](/en/coree-du-sud) gives it, and it sits next to the rules for the eight other countries on this site.

## What you are actually buying, if you buy one

One thing: **a valid K-ETA exempts you from filling in the paper arrival card** handed out on the plane.

That is the entire benefit for someone already exempt from needing it. It is worth 10 000 won to some people — those connecting tight, those travelling with children, those who would rather not write an address on a tray table. It is worth nothing to most.

**What it does not buy:** a faster queue, a guarantee of entry, or any right you did not already have. Only the country's immigration officer decides on entry, and they decide at the desk.

## Where to apply, and where not to

**`k-eta.go.kr`, and nowhere else.**

The K-ETA is among the most heavily resold travel authorisations there is. Intermediary sites charge several times the official fee for the identical form, and they buy the search results above the government page — which means the official portal is often not the first thing you see.

The defence is the same as for [Thailand's arrival card and the Philippines' eTravel](/en/blog/the-fees-that-are-not-the-visa): reach the portal from your own government's Korea travel advice page, never from a search result.

## The decision, in one line

**If your passport is waived and you are not desperate to skip a paper form, do not buy it.**

If you do buy it, buy it from the portal, and know before you click that the 10 000 won is gone whatever happens next.

## What this does not settle

How long you may stay. That belongs to your passport too, and the answer differs more than people expect between neighbours — the same trip that fits comfortably on one passport can require a visa on another.
