**You can be visa-free and still owe money at the border.** The visa is the formality everyone budgets for; it is rarely the only one.

What follows is the set of charges and registrations that sit beside it — some free, some not, and three that are routinely sold to people who could do them for nothing. Every figure here is read from the government portal named at the foot of this page.

## The ones that cost money

**Bali's tourist levy — 150 000 IDR, on each entry.** Separate from any visa, payable every time you arrive in Bali, and collected regardless of how you entered Indonesia. It is neither a scam nor optional. It appears in almost no budget because it is not the visa, and because it is charged per entry rather than per trip: two arrivals in Bali mean two levies.

**The K-ETA, when you buy one you do not need — 10 000 won.** Several nationalities are currently exempt from the K-ETA entirely. Applying for it anyway is allowed and occasionally useful, because holding one exempts you from filling in the arrival card. But the fee is **non-refundable even if the application is refused**, which is the part nobody reads before paying.

**Extending a stay**, where it is possible at all — a separate subject, and [we costed it country by country](/en/blog/extending-your-stay-southeast-asia).

## The ones that are free, and get resold anyway

These three are the reason this page exists.

**Thailand's digital arrival card (TDAC).** Compulsory since 1 May 2025 for every entry by air, land or sea, to be completed in the three days before you arrive. **It is free.** The official portal is `tdac.immigration.go.th`. Sites charging for it are reselling a government form, and they buy the search results above it.

**The Philippines' eTravel registration.** Compulsory, free, and asked for twice — by the airline at check-in and again on arrival. It generates a QR code. The same resale economy has grown around it.

**Cambodia's e-arrival app.** Compulsory since 1 September 2024 for every arrival by plane. Free on the official application.

**The defence is the same for all three, and it is worth a sentence.** Reach the portal from your own government's travel advice page for that country, never from a search result. The official page is rarely the first result, because the first result is paid for.

## The one that is not a fee but costs more than any of them

**China: the payment link, not the visa.** The country has largely stopped accepting cash. Alipay and WeChat Pay both accept foreign cards now — but the linking has to be done and tested before you fly, because the app stores that would let you fix it are themselves filtered once you land.

Nobody charges you for this. It costs a great deal if you discover it at the airport. [The full pre-departure sequence](/en/blog/arriving-in-china-preparation) covers it with the VPN and the train booking window.

## What the visa fee itself hides

Two figures worth knowing before you assume a quoted price is the price.

**Cambodia: 36 USD through the official portal, 40 USD in cash at land border posts.** The gap is small; arriving at a land crossing with only a card is not.

**Laos prices its visa on arrival by nationality**, between 30 and 50 USD depending on the passport. It is unusual enough that "what does a Lao visa cost" genuinely has no single answer — and it is one more case where a number without a passport attached means nothing.

## How to budget for all of this

Add a line to your trip budget called **formalities**, and put in it: the visa if you need one, the levy if you are going to Bali, and 20 to 30 € of margin.

The margin is not for the fees above — those are known and published. It is for the third card reader that does not work, the photograph you have to get taken at the airport, and the crossing that turns out to take cash.

## Before any of it

Every formality on this page sits on top of an entry rule, and **that rule belongs to your passport**. The same country can be visa-free for 45 days on one and require a visa from day one on another.

[Check yours](/en/do-i-need-a-visa) before you budget anything: the answer arrives with the government source it was read from and the month it was read.
