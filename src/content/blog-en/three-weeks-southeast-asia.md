Three weeks is the most common length and the worst used. The temptation is to tick off four countries; the result is a trip spent in transit.

Here is a three-country itinerary that works, with real travel times — and, at the end, the two stops I would cut if I did it again.

## The itinerary, 21 days

**Days 1–4 — Hanoi and Ninh Binh.** Two days in Hanoi to land: the old quarter on foot, a balcony café, street food. Then two days in Ninh Binh, two hours away — the same karst pinnacles as Ha Long bay, without the boats, by rowing boat through the paddies. [Which district to sleep in](/en/blog/where-to-stay-hanoi) decides the first two nights.

**Days 5–7 — Lan Ha bay.** Out of Hai Phong rather than the main Ha Long port. The same landscape, a tenth of the traffic. Two nights on Cat Ba, one day cruise.

**Days 8–10 — Hoi An.** Fly Hanoi to Da Nang, an hour and twenty. Hoi An early in the morning and in the evening; the afternoon on the beach or cycling through the paddies. The old town between 11:00 and 16:00 holds no particular interest. If you go between September and December, [read this first](/en/blog/hoi-an-when-it-rains) — a shower and a flood are not the same thing.

**Days 11–14 — Siem Reap and Angkor.** Fly Da Nang to Siem Reap, with a connection. Three full days at Angkor, in the order described in [our Angkor article](/en/blog/angkor-in-the-rainy-season): the outlying temples first, the famous ones after.

**Days 15–17 — Bangkok.** Fly Siem Reap to Bangkok, one hour. Bangkok by boat along the klongs, not by taxi. One day for Ayutthaya by bicycle, by train from the city. [Where you stay](/en/blog/where-to-stay-bangkok) is a question of which station, not which district.

**Days 18–21 — the gulf, or Kanchanaburi.** Two options depending on the season. November to September: Koh Tao or Koh Phangan, four days with no plan. In October and November, when the gulf takes on water: Kanchanaburi and the Erawan park, three hours from Bangkok.

## Travel times, without illusions

| Leg | Real door-to-door time |
| --- | --- |
| Hanoi → Ninh Binh | 2 h 30 by private car |
| Ninh Binh → Cat Ba | 4 h including the ferry |
| Hanoi → Da Nang | 4 h with airport transfers |
| Da Nang → Siem Reap | 6 to 9 h with a connection |
| Siem Reap → Bangkok | 4 h with transfers |
| Bangkok → Koh Tao | 9 to 11 h (bus plus night ferry) |

**Total: about three full days of transport out of twenty-one.** That is the irreducible minimum for three countries. Every stop you add costs half a day more.

## The budget

Per person, in modest comfort:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Accommodation, 20 nights | 420 € |
| Food and drink | 290 € |
| Internal flights (2) | 110 € |
| Overland transport | 130 € |
| Activities, including the Angkor pass | 250 € |
| Other | 80 € |
| **Total on the ground** | **≈ 1,280 €** |

Plus the international flight, plus insurance at 45 to 70 €. Amounts recorded in euros — convert at the rate of the day.

## What to cut

**Hoi An, or rather its length.** Three nights is one too many. The town is magnificent for two hours in the morning and two in the evening; in between it is saturated. Two nights are enough, and the day you save goes to Ninh Binh or to Angkor.

**Bangkok as a three-day stop.** Bangkok deserves a proper visit, but not in the middle of a loaded itinerary, arriving tired from two countries. Two nights in transit, or else four days at the start of the trip, when you have the energy.

**What to do with the days you save:** not add a stop. Put them into the stops you already have. Staying four nights somewhere changes the nature of the trip — you start recognising faces, you understand the rhythm of the town, you go back to eat where it was good.

## The rule that applies to every itinerary

**One stop per three days, maximum.** Twenty-one days means seven stops at the outside, and six is better. Beyond that, every day gained in places seen is lost to travel days — and it is not the places seen that you talk about when you get home.

## The paperwork, one country at a time

There is no single answer for a three-country route, because each entry rule belongs to a passport rather than to a country, and the three schemes are unrelated. Check each of them for your own passport before you book anything non-refundable: [Vietnam](/en/vietnam), [Cambodia](/en/cambodge), [Thailand](/en/thailande). Each page gives the rule, the government source it came from, and the month it was read.
