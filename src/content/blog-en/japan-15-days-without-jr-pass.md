The JR Pass is the most automatic reflex of a trip to Japan, and since the successive price rises it has become the wrong one. Here is the sum, applied to a complete itinerary.

## The sum, first

Take the itinerary below: Tokyo → Matsumoto → Takayama → Kanazawa → Kyoto → Tokyo, over fifteen days.

The long-distance legs come to five segments. Bought individually, at the standard fares for those lines, the total sits around **28,000 to 34,000 yen**.

A two-week JR Pass costs considerably more than that since the fare revision. And part of this itinerary — the Takayama line, some of the alpine buses — is not covered by the pass anyway.

**The practical conclusion:** do the sum on *your* itinerary, with real fares, before you buy. The pass wins again in one case only — if you chain Tokyo, Hiroshima, Kyushu and back within the same week. On classic routes, no. [We did the calculation leg by leg](/en/blog/jr-pass-worth-it) on several common itineraries.

One non-financial argument survives in the pass's favour: flexibility. You board any train, without reserving, without thinking. If your trip is deliberately improvised, that has value. If your route is fixed, it has none.

## The itinerary, fifteen days

**Days 1–4 — Tokyo.** One district a day, no more. Yanaka and Nezu on the first day, for a soft landing. Shibuya and Shimokitazawa on the second. The third for a museum and Kiyosumi. The fourth for whatever the first three brought to light.

**Day 5 — Tokyo → Matsumoto.** The black castle, one of the country's few original keeps. A pleasant, quiet town, and a good transition into the mountains.

**Days 6–7 — Kamikochi and the Japanese Alps.** The Kamikochi valley is closed to cars and reached by bus. Easy walking along the Azusa, mountains at 3,000 metres. This is the Japan nobody puts on a poster.

**Days 8–9 — Takayama and Shirakawa-go.** A wooden old town, a morning market, sake breweries. Shirakawa-go as a day trip, early: the thatched houses are worth the detour, the eleven o'clock coaches much less so.

**Days 10–11 — Kanazawa.** The Kenrokuen garden, the Higashi Chaya geisha district, and the Omicho market. The city most itineraries skip, and which is worth Kyoto without the crowd.

**Days 12–14 — Kyoto.** Three days, and one rule that changes everything: **be out before eight in the morning, or after five in the afternoon.** The Philosopher's Path at 07:00 and at 14:00 are not the same place. Fushimi Inari at dawn is silent; at 11:00 it is a queue.

**Day 15 — back to Tokyo, or fly from Osaka.** Check both before booking: a return flight from Kansai saves an entire journey.

## Three decisions that change the trip

**Do not sleep near Kyoto station.** It is the natural reflex and it is a mistake: the city is far more spread out than it looks, and the interesting districts are north. [Where to stay in Kyoto](/en/blog/where-to-stay-kyoto) sets out the areas that actually work.

**Carry cash.** Japan is a very modern country that still runs largely on notes in small restaurants, temples and neighbourhood shops. Convenience store ATMs take foreign cards — that is the most reliable network.

**Do Visit Japan Web the night before you fly.** The online immigration and customs declaration saves a real half-hour on arrival, at a moment when you will not feel like anything.

## The budget

Over fifteen days, travelling in comfort — business hotels, two meals out a day, a few museums:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Accommodation | 70–90 € a night, or 1,050–1,350 € |
| Food | 30–40 € a day, or 450–600 € |
| Long-distance transport | 180–220 € |
| Urban transport and entry fees | 250–350 € |

So roughly **2,000 to 2,500 € per person** on the ground, excluding flights. That is expensive, but the gap with the country's reputation comes almost entirely from accommodation: Japanese food, at equal quality, is not the costly part. Amounts recorded in euros — convert at the rate of the day.

## One thing to check before booking

How long you may stay, which belongs to your passport rather than to Japan in the abstract. Many nationalities get 90 days visa-free on arrival; not all do. The [Japan page](/en/japon) gives the rule for yours, with the source and the month it was read.
