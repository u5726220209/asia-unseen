Most budgets published online stop at a figure per day. It is the least useful number there is: it does not say where the money goes, so it does not say what to change.

What follows is the full breakdown of a month in Vietnam in modest comfort — private rooms at 15 to 25 €, meals mostly from the street and from local restaurants, overland transport plus two internal flights, a handful of paid activities.

**A note on currency.** These are amounts actually spent, recorded in euros. Convert at the rate of the day rather than trusting a figure that was converted once and then aged.

## The total

**About 1,640 € on the ground for thirty days**, or **54.70 € a day**. Excluding the international flight and excluding insurance.

A forecast built on the standard ranges would have said 1,400 €. The 17 % gap is not an arithmetic error: it is the margin every travel budget needs and none includes.

## The breakdown

| Item | Amount | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accommodation (30 nights) | 585 € | 36 % |
| Food and drink | 410 € | 25 % |
| Long-distance transport | 245 € | 15 % |
| Local transport | 95 € | 6 % |
| Activities and entry fees | 210 € | 13 % |
| Other (SIM, laundry, pharmacy) | 95 € | 6 % |

## The three items forecasts get wrong

**Long-distance transport, by a distance.** Vietnam is 1,650 km north to south, and that is the number nobody builds in. Two internal flights — Hanoi to Da Nang, Da Nang to Saigon — cost 45 to 55 € each, on top of trains and buses. A typical forecast writes down 150 €; the actual came to 245 €.

Those flights are the right call all the same: each one replaces fifteen hours of train. They simply need budgeting rather than discovering. If you would rather do at least one leg on rails, [the Hanoi–Saigon train](/en/blog/hanoi-saigon-train) has the operator's own fare table.

**Drinks.** 410 € of food and drink, a meaningful share of it coffee and beer. A banh mi costs under a euro, a pho about two — Vietnamese food is extraordinarily cheap. A coffee in a well-kept café in Hanoi costs three euros, and you drink two a day. That is where the difference lives.

**Activities, concentrated in two or three lines.** 210 € against the 140 € of an average forecast. The Ha Giang loop alone accounts for 150 € of it. This is the general rule: two or three large activities absorb half the leisure budget and the rest scatters into small sums. Identify those two or three in advance and leave the remainder floating.

## What cost less than expected

**Accommodation.** 585 € for thirty nights, or 19.50 € a night — for private rooms, clean, often with breakfast. Vietnamese accommodation remains, with Laos, the best value in Asia.

What pulls that average down is booking as you go rather than in advance: four nights where you feel good, one where you do not. Which district you pick matters more than the hotel — [our Hanoi guide](/en/blog/where-to-stay-hanoi) makes the case.

**Local transport.** 95 € over a month using ride-hailing apps. The taxi negotiation disappears and the price you pay is the one quoted when you order.

## The costs that appear in no budget

**Bank charges.** With a card that does not add a currency conversion fee, six withdrawals over a month cost about a dozen euros. With an ordinary high-street card — 2 to 3 % on the exchange plus 4 to 6 € per withdrawal — the same thing comes to 65 or 80 €. It is the best return available on one hour of preparation before departure.

**Airport exchange counters.** The rate at the counter in Hanoi's airport is 5 to 8 % worse than in the city. On 900 € changed, that is 45 to 70 € — two hotel nights, gone in three minutes on the way out of the terminal. [Getting into Hanoi from the airport](/en/blog/hanoi-airport-to-city) has the bus fares, which you can pay in cash changed later.

## The three corrections to make

**Fewer stops.** Eleven towns in thirty days is two too many. Every change costs half a day between packing, travelling and settling in again — and the places where you stay four nights repay more than the ones you pass through.

**Book the north–south internal flight early.** Bought five days out it costs 55 €; booked three weeks ahead, around 30 €. Across two flights, the difference pays for two nights.

**Take the large items out of the daily budget.** Ha Giang, Lan Ha bay, a site pass: three lines that account for 40 % of the activities budget. Budgeting them separately stops you believing 45 € a day will do.

## The formula that works

To estimate a Vietnam budget, the rule that held up: **daily budget × number of days, plus the large items identified separately, plus 15 %.**

Over thirty days at a base of 45 €/day (1,350 €), plus 150 € of large items, plus 15 %, you land on 1,725 €. The actual was 1,640 €. That is an estimate you can use.

## One thing that changes the length of the trip

How long you may stay is not the same for everyone: British travellers currently get 45 days visa-free, Canadians need a visa from day one. Over a thirty-day plan, that difference is the whole question. The [Vietnam page](/en/vietnam) gives the rule for your passport, with its source and the month it was read.
