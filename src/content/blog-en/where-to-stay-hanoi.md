**In Hanoi, two hotels at the same price two kilometres apart give you two different trips.** The city is dense, loud, and organised in a way that makes the district decision matter more than the hotel's star rating.

## The Old Quarter — for a first visit

Thirty-six streets, each historically dedicated to a trade, and the highest density of anything you came for: food, markets, the lake ten minutes away on foot.

**It is genuinely loud.** Scooters, karaoke and deliveries run late and start early. This is not a detail you can plan around by picking a nicer hotel. Ask for a room that does not face the street, or read the next section.

## The French Quarter — for actually sleeping

Ten minutes' walk south, wide avenues, colonial buildings, the opera house. The same city, at a third of the volume.

**Who it suits:** light sleepers, second visits, and anyone who wants to be able to hear themselves think at the end of the day. Nobody regrets the ten-minute walk; plenty of people regret their first night in the Old Quarter.

## Ba Dinh and Truc Bach — the balance

Between the two in noise and in price, with the mausoleum area and a lake of its own. Fewer restaurants within stumbling distance, more space for the money.

## Tay Ho — for staying rather than visiting

The expatriate district on the West Lake. Larger rooms, cafés built for laptops, and a genuinely pleasant lakeside.

**The trade-off is distance.** Allow 20 to 30 minutes to the centre, more at peak hours. Over a fortnight of remote work, excellent. Over three nights of sightseeing, a mistake.

## The area to avoid outright

**Anything near the airport.** There is nothing there, the city is 45 minutes away, and you will spend on transfers what you saved on the room.

The single exception: a flight before six in the morning, on the last night. Never on the first.

## Getting in from the airport

Bus 86 runs to the railway station for 50,000 dong, a short ride from the Old Quarter. The ordinary routes cost 12,000 to 15,000 dong but stop further out. **There is no train or metro to Noi Bai** — the full route-by-route list, with the operator's own fares, is in [our Hanoi airport guide](/en/blog/hanoi-airport-to-city).

If you are arriving or leaving by rail, [the Hanoi–Saigon train](/en/blog/hanoi-saigon-train) leaves from Hanoi station, which is walkable from both the Old and the French Quarter.

## One thing to sort out before you book

How long you may stay, which is not the same for everyone. British travellers currently get 45 days visa-free; Canadians need a visa before departure, from day one. The [Vietnam page](/en/vietnam) gives the rule for your passport, with its source and the month it was read.

## The method, in three minutes

1. **Decide whether you sleep lightly.** If yes, the French Quarter. If no, the Old Quarter.
2. **Count your nights.** Under five, stay central. Over ten, Tay Ho becomes defensible.
3. **Check the walk to Hoan Kiem lake.** Beyond fifteen minutes, you are further out than you think.
