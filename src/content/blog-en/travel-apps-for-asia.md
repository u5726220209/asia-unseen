Lists of "30 essential travel apps" are a genre of their own, and they are all wrong: nobody uses thirty apps while travelling. Here are the six that genuinely do work, one per job, in the order to install them.

## 1. Ride-hailing — one per region

This is the app that changes daily life most. The price is shown in advance, there is no negotiation, no doctored meter, and the route is recorded.

| Region | App |
| --- | --- |
| Southeast Asia | Grab |
| Indonesia | Gojek (or Grab) |
| China | DiDi |
| South Korea | Kakao T |
| Japan | GO |

**The prerequisite:** a phone number for verification. Some accept a home number, others insist on a local one — all the more reason to sort out your data before you fly.

In Southeast Asia, Grab also does food delivery, which is useful on the evening you arrive with energy for nothing.

## 2. Maps, offline

Google Maps works everywhere except mainland China. Two caveats that matter:

- **Download the offline areas before you go.** It halves or thirds your data use and works in valleys with no signal — in Laos, in the Visayas or on the Ha Giang loop, that happens often.
- **In South Korea**, Google Maps is restricted for regulatory reasons: walking directions and transport are very incomplete. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap.

For hiking and back roads, an offline topographic map app does better than Google Maps, which ignores most trails.

## 3. The translator, with the language downloaded

The decisive use is not conversation: it is **text recognition through the camera**. Reading a menu, a station board, a medicine leaflet, a price label.

Download the offline language pack before you leave. In China and Japan it is probably the single most useful tool of the trip.

## 4. Overland transport

To book trains, buses and ferries in Southeast Asia, one aggregator does the job properly: [12Go](https://12go.asia). Real timetables, named operators, tickets in English — which local ticket windows do not always provide.

In Japan and Korea the national rail apps are ample. In China, high-speed reservations go through the official platforms within a fifteen-day window: that is the point not to miss, and [we covered it in full](/en/blog/china-train-booking-window).

## 5. Payment — the Chinese case

In Southeast Asia, Japan and Korea, your bank card and a little cash are enough.

**China is a total exception.** The country runs on mobile payment — Alipay or WeChat Pay — including with small traders and on the metro. Both now accept foreign cards, but **the linking must be done and tested from home**. Arriving without it means paying cash in a country that barely uses it. [The full pre-departure sequence](/en/blog/arriving-in-china-preparation) is worth reading a fortnight out.

## 6. A VPN — for China only

Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram and most Western services are unreachable from mainland China. The app stores are filtered too: **a VPN downloaded once you are there is a VPN you will not download**.

To do before departure:

1. Subscribe and install on the phone **and** the laptop.
2. Test it by connecting to a server outside China.
3. Install a second one, from another provider — VPNs are blocked regularly.
4. Note the credentials somewhere other than a password manager that would need to sync.

Outside China, a VPN is not needed day to day.

## What does nothing

**Budget-tracking apps.** They add friction where a note on your phone — one line per expense, category and amount — does the job in five seconds. What matters is writing it down as you pay, not the tool.

**Currency converters.** Memorise a mental rule instead: in Vietnam, drop the last four zeros and divide by two and a half for the order of magnitude in euros. Faster than pulling out a phone at every price.

**Real-time voice translation.** It works badly in noise, which is to say everywhere you would need it. Text and camera do better.

**Paid guidebook apps.** The content is older than what you will find online, and it is not updated.

## The checklist, the night before

- [ ] Regional ride-hailing app installed, account created and verified
- [ ] Offline map areas downloaded for every stop
- [ ] Translator language pack downloaded
- [ ] eSIM installed but **not yet activated**
- [ ] Data roaming switched off on your home line
- [ ] China only: VPN tested, Alipay linked and tested
- [ ] Passport, visas and insurance photographed and emailed to yourself

That last point is not an app, and it is the most useful item on the list.
