**A Chinese train ticket goes on sale fifteen days before departure, and not a minute earlier.** Beyond that, the train is not full: it does not exist in the system yet.

This is the constraint nobody anticipates on the best high-speed network in the world, and it cannot be worked around. It does, however, change how you build a trip.

## The rule, as published

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Sales open | **15 days** before departure |
| Sales close | **30 minutes** before departure |
| Platform service hours | **05:00 to 01:00**, Beijing time |
| Passports accepted | those recognised by China, on the English site |

Concretely: a ticket for the 30th opens on the 16th. A ticket for the 15th opens on the 1st.

Between 01:00 and 05:00 Beijing time the platform is under maintenance and **nothing sells**. For a busy route on a peak day, the fifteenth-day opening therefore falls in the middle of the night wherever you are.

## What it imposes on an itinerary

**Your train legs cannot be locked in when you buy your flights.** That is the whole difference with Japan or Korea, where people book months ahead.

Two practical consequences.

**The first is reassuring.** On the vast majority of routes, capacity is such that a ticket remains available two or three days out. The Chinese network runs trains every ten minutes on the main corridors: the fear of “not getting a seat” is almost always unfounded outside festival periods.

**The second is less so.** There are dates when everything goes in minutes: Spring Festival, the Golden Week of early October, and long weekends. If your trip crosses one of them, the fifteen-day window becomes an appointment you cannot miss, and you need to be at the screen when it opens.

What to do, concretely: **build the shape of the trip without train tickets, and buy as you go.** That is how China normally works, not a fallback.

## The agencies that “book” earlier

**No agency can obtain a ticket before the official release.** Not the biggest, not the most expensive.

What they sell is a place in a queue: they record your request, then buy the ticket the second it opens, for a service fee. It is neither illegal nor dishonest — it is simply a service you can perform for yourself, for nothing, by setting a reminder on your phone.

The one case where it defends itself: a departure on a national holiday, at an hour when you cannot be available. Paying someone to click at 4 a.m. is a reasonable trade. Saying so is not in our interest — we earn nothing on these services — but it is true.

## The ticket is personal, and that is the trap

**Every ticket is tied to a passport number**, and identity verification is required before any purchase. Three consequences people discover too late.

The passport you register must be **the one you travel on**. A renewal between purchase and departure invalidates the ticket.

At the station, platform access is by passport, not by a paper ticket. Automatic gates read Chinese ID cards; **a foreign passport goes through a staffed counter or a manual check**, which takes time. Plan to arrive earlier than your European or American habit.

Finally, the app works in English but **payment with a foreign card remains the most common point of friction**. Test it once on a short, cheap journey before depending on it for a connection that matters.

## What this page does not tell you

Entry rules are not the same for every passport. British travellers currently enter China visa-free for up to 30 days under a scheme running to 31 December 2026; Canadians are included in the same waiver. Other nationalities are not.

## What not to do

**Buy domestic flights out of fear of the train.** On the main corridors, high-speed rail beats flying city centre to city centre, and the fifteen-day window is not a real handicap outside festival periods.

**Leave it to the last day for a festival departure.** Golden Week in early October and Spring Festival are the two moments when the rule really bites.

**Book through an intermediary without knowing what you are buying.** The service is a queue. It can be worth its price; it is never worth believing you hold a ticket when you hold a promise.

**Arrive at the station as you would at home.** Identity check, security, and a foreign passport that does not pass the automatic gates: allow plenty of time.

## In short

- Tickets open **15 days** ahead, close **30 minutes** before departure.
- Nothing sells between **01:00 and 05:00**, Beijing time.
- The ticket is **personal** and tied to your passport.
- An agency cannot book earlier: it sells a place in a queue.
