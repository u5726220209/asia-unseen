There are two ways to ride the Ha Giang loop, and choosing between them is the only genuinely important decision of these four days.

## First, the question of who steers

The loop is about 350 kilometres of mountain road in the far north of Vietnam, against the Chinese border. Passes at 1,500 metres, hairpins with no barrier, gravel sections, and lorries that take their share of the road.

**If you have no real experience of motorcycling in mountains, take an easy rider.** These are local riders, often young, who carry you as a passenger for the whole loop. You look at the landscape instead of the tarmac, and you arrive in one piece.

This is not excessive caution. Hospitals in the region are not equipped for serious trauma, and an evacuation to Hanoi is six to seven hours by road. Without a valid motorcycle licence and an international driving permit, no insurance will cover anything at all.

If you do ride, film the bike before you leave, from every angle, in front of the person hiring it to you. That single reflex settles 90 % of disputes on return.

## The route in four days

**Day 1 — Ha Giang → Yen Minh (100 km).** The climb starts immediately. Stop at the Bac Sum pass, which sets the tone: within the first hour you understand why people make this trip. A deliberately short day, to get used to the road.

**Day 2 — Yen Minh → Dong Van (60 km).** The finest day, and the shortest in distance. The Dong Van karst plateau is a UNESCO global geopark and it shows. Take in the Hmong king's palace at Sa Phin. The Dong Van market, on Sunday morning, is worth building your schedule around.

**Day 3 — Dong Van → Du Gia, over the Ma Pi Leng pass (110 km).** The pass is the high point of the trip in both senses: the road is cut into the cliff above the Nho Que river, 800 metres below. Allow time for a boat trip down the river — an hour, a few euros, and a completely different perspective.

**Day 4 — Du Gia → Ha Giang (80 km).** Back through the valleys, greener, more agricultural, less spectacular and more restful. The Du Gia waterfall in the morning, before the heat.

## The real cost, line by line

For four days, per person, riding yourself:

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Night bus Hanoi–Ha Giang, return | 30–40 € |
| Motorbike hire (semi-automatic) | 40–60 € |
| Fuel | 12–18 € |
| Homestays, 3 nights | 18–30 € |
| Food and drink | 25–35 € |
| Border zone permit | 2–4 € |
| **Total** | **130–190 €** |

With an easy rider, add 100 to 160 €: the rider, the bike and the fuel are included, and he will negotiate the homestays better than you will. Amounts recorded in euros — convert at the rate of the day.

## What is not said often enough

**The homestays are the main point of the trip.** Shared dormitories on mattresses on the floor, a meal eaten with the family, rice spirit you will not be able to decline. It is not comfortable; it is better than that.

**You need a border zone permit** for the sections near China. It is issued in Ha Giang, costs a few tens of thousands of dong, and takes ten minutes. Agencies and easy riders handle it; riding solo, do not forget it.

**Fog changes everything.** At 1,500 metres a day can go from a 40-kilometre panorama to twenty metres of visibility in half an hour. That does not matter if you have a spare day. It matters a great deal if your flight leaves tomorrow.

**Four days is the right length.** In three you ride constantly. In five you stop in places where there is not much. Four is the rhythm that leaves time to stop when stopping is worth it.

## The two adjustments people make most often

**Do not book the homestays in advance.** It is the first-timer's reflex, from fear of finding nothing. Outside high-season weekends it is unnecessary — and it removes exactly the flexibility that makes the loop worth doing: staying an extra night where you are happy, cutting short where you are not.

**Set your departure around the Dong Van Sunday market.** Leaving on a Thursday rather than a Monday changes nothing about the logistics and drops you there at the right moment. It is the kind of detail that transforms a whole day.
