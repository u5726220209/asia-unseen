**The length written on your arrival stamp is not always final.** In most of Southeast Asia a tourist stay can be extended at an immigration desk, for a published fee. In one country on this list it cannot, and that fact should shape the trip rather than be discovered in week three.

What follows is the official fee in each country, with the portal it was read from. **The length you are granted in the first place depends on your passport** — that is a separate question, and each country page answers it for yours.

## Where it can be done, and what it costs

| Country | Extension | Official fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thailand | Once, at an immigration office | 1 900 THB |
| Philippines | 29 days, to 59 in total | ≈ 3 030 PHP |
| Laos | Per day, in Vientiane | 2 USD per day |
| Cambodia | Through the visa itself, not an extension | — |
| Indonesia | Through the visa itself, not an extension | — |
| Vietnam | **Not possible** | — |

Two of those rows say something other than a number, and the difference matters.

## Thailand: one extension, at a counter

**1 900 THB, once**, at an immigration office. It is a counter formality rather than a new visa — you turn up, you pay, you are stamped.

Three things people get wrong about it:

**It is granted, not owed.** The officer can refuse, and occasionally does, most often when the stay already looks like residence rather than tourism.

**Go early.** Several days before your stamp expires, not on the last day. An office closed for a public holiday you did not know about turns a formality into an overstay.

**The entry rule changed in September 2026**, and the length you start from is not what it was. The [Thailand page](/en/thailande) gives the current rule for your passport, with the date it switched.

## The Philippines: 29 days, and a published price

**About 3 030 PHP** buys a 29-day extension, taking a typical tourist stay to 59 days in total. It is done at a Bureau of Immigration office.

The fee being published is the useful part. Extensions in the Philippines are a well-known point of friction with fixers offering to "handle it", and the only defence is knowing the number before you walk in.

## Laos: by the day

**2 USD per day**, in Vientiane. It is the most flexible arrangement on this list and the most easily misread: it is not a fixed package, it is a daily rate, so a three-week extension costs three weeks of it.

Worth knowing alongside it: **the visa on arrival itself is priced by nationality** in Laos, between 30 and 50 USD depending on the passport. That is unusual, and it is one more reason the question "what does a Lao visa cost" has no single answer.

## Cambodia and Indonesia: the visa does the work

Neither country runs a simple counter extension of a visa-free stay. What they have instead is a visa whose length is chosen at the point of application.

In Cambodia the tourist e-visa costs **36 USD including service fees** through the official portal, and **40 USD in cash at land border posts** — a gap worth knowing before you arrive at one with only a card.

In Indonesia, the arrangement that matters to most visitors is the electronic visa on arrival, and the point of friction is elsewhere: **the Bali tourist levy of 150 000 IDR**, payable on each entry, separate from any visa.

## Vietnam: the one that cannot be extended

**Neither the visa exemption nor the e-visa is extendable on the spot.**

This is the single most consequential line on the page, because it inverts the planning. Everywhere else, a trip that runs long can be fixed at a desk. In Vietnam it cannot: the days have to exist before you fly.

If your plan needs more than the exemption grants you, the e-visa is applied for from home, before departure. [Ten days in the north](/en/blog/vietnam-in-10-days) fits comfortably inside any of these rules; [a month](/en/blog/one-month-in-vietnam-cost) does not fit inside all of them.

## What overstaying actually costs

A daily fine, paid on the way out, and in several countries a record that can affect later entries.

It is never a quiet formality. It is paid **at the airport, in cash, at the moment you are trying to leave** — which is the worst possible moment to discover the amount, and the reason this page exists.

## The rule that comes before all of this

Every number above is an extension fee. **How long you are granted in the first place belongs to your passport**, not to the country in the abstract: the same country can give 45 days to one passport and require a visa from day one on another.

Check yours before you book anything non-refundable: [Thailand](/en/thailande), [Laos](/en/laos), [Cambodia](/en/cambodge), [the Philippines](/en/philippines), [Indonesia](/en/indonesie), [Vietnam](/en/vietnam).
