The most common mistake on ten days in Vietnam is trying to do Hanoi, Hoi An and Saigon. The country is 1,650 km north to south: that plan means two internal flights, three hotel check-ins, and the permanent sensation of being about to leave.

Here is the alternative: one region, the north, in depth.

## The itinerary, day by day

**Days 1–2 — Hanoi.** Arrive, sleep, plan nothing on the first day. The old quarter is best walked without an itinerary. Second day: the ethnology museum in the morning — the only one in the country that genuinely explains the 54 ethnic groups — and a balcony café late in the afternoon. Which district you sleep in matters more than the hotel: [our guide covers it](/en/blog/where-to-stay-hanoi).

**Days 3–4 — Ninh Binh.** Two hours south. The same karst pinnacles as Ha Long bay, planted in rice paddies and toured by rowing boat. Tam Coc is the better known, Trang An the more beautiful. Hire a bicycle: the loop between Hoa Lu and Bich Dong is 20 flat kilometres between the cliffs.

**Days 5–8 — the Ha Giang loop.** The heart of the trip. A night bus from Hanoi, then four days of mountain against the Chinese border. [The full account](/en/blog/ha-giang-loop-four-days) has the route, the costs and the handlebar question.

One thing to settle before you go: if you have no real experience of riding a motorbike in mountains, take an *easy rider* and travel as a passenger. You will watch the landscape instead of the tarmac, and you will come back in one piece.

**Days 9–10 — Lan Ha bay.** Back to Hanoi, then out towards Hai Phong rather than the main Ha Long port. The same landscape, a tenth of the boats. One night on Cat Ba, a day cruise, and an unhurried return to Hanoi for the flight.

## The real travel times

| Leg | Door to door |
| --- | --- |
| Hanoi → Ninh Binh | 2 h 30 by private car |
| Ninh Binh → Hanoi | 2 h 30 |
| Hanoi → Ha Giang | 6 to 7 h (night bus) |
| Ha Giang → Hanoi | 6 to 7 h (night bus) |
| Hanoi → Cat Ba | 3 h 30 including the ferry |
| Cat Ba → Hanoi | 3 h 30 |

Both Ha Giang legs run overnight: they cost no daylight at all. That is what makes this itinerary hold together in ten days.

## The budget

Per person, in modest comfort:

| Item | Amount |

| Accommodation, 9 nights | 175 € |
| Food and drink | 130 € |
| Ha Giang loop, all in | 160 € |
| Transport (bus, ferry, Ninh Binh car) | 95 € |
| Lan Ha cruise | 75 € |

Plus the international flight and insurance, 30 to 45 € for ten days. Amounts recorded in euros; the full month-by-month breakdown is in [our thirty-day Vietnam budget](/en/blog/one-month-in-vietnam-cost).

## Why this beats a north–south run

**Three unpackings instead of six.** Every change of town costs half a day between packing, travelling and settling in. Here you open your bag three times.

**No internal flights.** No airport transfer, no check-in, no delay. Over ten days that is a whole day handed back.

**A real progression.** City, countryside, mountain, sea. No stage resembles the one before — which is not true of three large cities in a row.

## A technical trap on the official portal

Checked on 30 August 2026, and it deserves a paragraph of its own.

The portal at `evisa.gov.vn` serves an **incomplete certificate chain**: the server presents its own certificate but not the intermediate that links it to a recognised authority. Depending on your browser and device, that shows up as a security warning — "your connection is not private", "certificate not verified".

**The certificate is entirely legitimate.** It is issued by GlobalSign in the name of `evisa.gov.vn`, for the Vietnam Immigration Department. The fault is a server configuration error, not an impersonation.

The problem is what the warning causes: the traveller concludes the official site is fraudulent, closes the tab, and goes back to the first sponsored result — an intermediary who will charge 60 to 120 € for a 25 USD formality. **The security warning pushes people towards the scam instead of protecting them from it.**

**What to do:** check that the domain is exactly `evisa.gov.vn`, with no subdomain and no suffix. If the warning appears, it is still the official site. If you would rather take no chances at all, reach it through the link on your own government's Vietnam travel advice page, which points at the same domain.

## What to settle before you leave

- **Your entry rule**, which belongs to your passport rather than to the country. The [Vietnam page](/en/vietnam) gives it, with the government source and the month it was read.
- **The border zone permit** for Ha Giang, obtained locally in ten minutes.
- **The season**: October to April for the north. September and October for golden rice terraces, March and April for blossom.
- **Insurance**, especially if you intend to ride at Ha Giang: without the right licence category and an international permit, nothing covers you.

## And if you have fourteen days

Add **Cao Bang and the Ban Gioc falls** after Ha Giang — two more days, almost no Western travellers. Or go south to **Hue and Hoi An** on the night train, which gives the trip a genuine second chapter without turning it into a race. [The Hanoi–Saigon line](/en/blog/hanoi-saigon-train) covers that section, and [Hoi An in the rain](/en/blog/hoi-an-when-it-rains) matters if you go between September and December.

What not to do with four extra days: add Saigon. At the other end of the country, it deserves its own trip.
