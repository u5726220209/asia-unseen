**Most of what goes wrong on arrival in Bali has nothing to do with the visa.** The visa is the part everyone prepares. What catches people is a levy, a form, and the physical state of their passport.

All three are handled before you land, in about twenty minutes.

## 1. The tourist levy — 150 000 IDR, on each entry

Bali charges a tourist levy of **150 000 IDR**, separate from any visa and payable **on each entry**.

That last word is the one to notice. It is charged per arrival, not per trip: a side trip to Lombok or Singapore and back means paying it again. Nobody tells you this at the first desk.

**Pay it online before you land.** It is payable on arrival too, but Denpasar's arrival hall is not a place you want to add a queue to — twenty minutes at home is twenty minutes, and twenty minutes there is an hour.

It appears in almost no travel budget, for a simple reason: it is not the visa, and budgets have a line for the visa. [The other fees that are not the visa](/en/blog/the-fees-that-are-not-the-visa) are worth reading in the same sitting.

## 2. The customs declaration — free, within 72 hours

Indonesia runs an **electronic customs declaration**, to be completed in the **72 hours before you arrive**. It produces a QR code, scanned on the way out of the baggage hall.

**It is free on the official portal.** It is also one of the formalities most reliably resold by intermediary sites, which buy the search results above the government page and charge the price of a meal for a form you fill in yourself.

The defence is the one that works everywhere: reach the portal from your own government's Indonesia travel advice page, not from a search result. The first result is first because someone paid for it.

## 3. The passport itself — and this is the one that ends trips

**A damaged passport can get you refused entry to Indonesia**, and the rule is applied more strictly there than most travellers expect.

A torn page. A water-stained cover. A laminate lifting at the corner of the photo page. None of these feel like a problem at home; all of them have been enough.

If your passport has taken any damage at all, **deal with it before you book**, not at the desk. This is the only item on this page that cannot be fixed once you are in the queue — the levy can be paid there, the form can be filled in on your phone, and a refused entry means a flight home.

## The order to do it in

1. **Check your passport's physical condition**, today, before anything else is booked. Replacement takes weeks.
2. **Check the entry rule for your passport** — it is not the same for everyone. The [Indonesia page](/en/indonesie) gives yours, with the government source and the month it was read.
3. **Apply for the visa** if your passport needs one, from the official portal.
4. **Fill in the customs declaration** in the three days before departure.
5. **Pay the levy online**, any time before you land.

## What this does not cover

The entry rule itself, which belongs to your passport and not to Indonesia in the abstract. Some nationalities use the electronic visa on arrival; others have different arrangements, and the length granted differs too.

That is the question the [Indonesia page](/en/indonesie) answers, and the one worth settling before the rest of this list matters at all. If you are pricing a trip rather than preparing one, [what a month in Southeast Asia costs](/en/guides/asia-travel-budget) puts Indonesia beside its neighbours.

## What not to do

**Assume the levy is a scam.** It is official, it is published, and refusing to pay it does not end well.

**Pay an intermediary for the customs form.** It is free, and the official portal takes five minutes.

**Travel on a passport with a lifting laminate** because it has always been fine before. In Indonesia, it is the one that has not.

**Count on paying everything at the airport.** You can. You will also spend an hour doing it, at the end of a long flight, in a hall built for fewer people than it receives.
