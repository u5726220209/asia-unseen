This itinerary rests on one idea: instead of flying between two countries, you cross the border by river. Two days on the Mekong at 12 km/h, between banks where there is nothing but forest and a few villages.

It is the opposite of a transfer. It is the stage you remember.

## The itinerary, 18 days

**Days 1–3 — Bangkok.** One day to land, two for the city. The rule that changes everything: move by boat on the Chao Phraya and the klongs, not by taxi. You will see the city from its river side and avoid three hours of traffic a day. [Where to stay](/en/blog/where-to-stay-bangkok) is a question of which station.

**Day 4 — Ayutthaya.** An hour by train from Bangkok, as a day trip or with a night there. The old capital is visited by bicycle, among brick ruins scattered through a town that is still very much alive. Go early: there is no shade at midday.

**Days 5–9 — Chiang Mai and the north.** Night train from Bangkok, second-class sleeper — a classic that still works. Three days in Chiang Mai: the old town temples, a cookery class that starts at the market, and Doi Suthep late in the afternoon. Then two days on the Mae Hong Son loop or towards Pai, depending on your appetite for bends.

**Day 10 — Chiang Rai and the border.** Bus to Chiang Rai, a night there. The white temple at first light, before the groups.

**Days 11–12 — the slow boat.** Border crossing at Chiang Khong to Huay Xai in the morning, then boarding. Two days of sailing, one night at Pak Beng. Bring something to read, water, and an inflatable cushion — the benches are wooden.

**Days 13–15 — Luang Prabang.** Arriving by river is the right way to enter this town. Three days: the Kuang Si falls, Mount Phousi at sunset, the morning market. The monks' alms round at dawn is watched from a distance, without flash, and without buying rice from the street vendors.

**Days 16–17 — Nong Khiaw.** Four hours north-east, in a valley hemmed in by limestone cliffs. The Pha Daeng viewpoint at first light, and a boat up to Muang Ngoi — a village reachable only by river.

**Day 18 — home.** Fly from Luang Prabang, or take the high-speed train to Vientiane if you are extending.

## The journeys, without illusions

| Leg | Real duration |
| --- | --- |
| Bangkok → Ayutthaya | 1 h 30 by train |
| Bangkok → Chiang Mai | 12 h on the night train |
| Chiang Mai → Chiang Rai | 3 h 30 by bus |
| Chiang Rai → Huay Xai (border) | 2 h 30, formalities included |
| Huay Xai → Luang Prabang | 2 days by boat, night at Pak Beng |
| Luang Prabang → Nong Khiaw | 4 h by minibus |

**Only three legs run over four hours**, and two of them are the attraction itself. That is what makes this route enjoyable where an ordinary chain of buses becomes exhausting.

## The border crossing, in practice

This is the part that worries people most and causes the fewest problems.

1. Bus or taxi from Chiang Rai to Chiang Khong.
2. Exit Thailand at the Friendship Bridge No. 4 post.
3. The compulsory shuttle across the bridge — a few tens of baht.
4. Enter Laos: e-visa presented, or visa on arrival. Bring US dollars in cash, in clean notes, and a passport photograph.
5. Tuk-tuk to the Huay Xai boat landing.

Allow the morning.

**The trap to know about:** the all-inclusive "Bangkok to Luang Prabang" tickets sold on tourist streets bundle bus, border and boat at a knock-down price — and put you on overloaded boats with a commission stop built in. Buy each segment separately, or go through an operator you can identify.

| Other | 60 € |
| **Total on the ground** | **≈ 1,050 €** |

Plus the international flight and insurance at 45 to 70 €. Amounts recorded in euros — convert at the rate of the day. The entry line is the one that varies most by passport: treat it as a placeholder until you have checked your own.

## When to go

**November to February.** Dry on both sides of the border, bearable temperatures, and the Mekong still high after the monsoon.

**Avoid March and early April.** The crop-burning season drowns all of northern Thailand and Laos in dense haze. Chiang Mai becomes hard to breathe in, and the Nong Khiaw panoramas disappear outright. It is the one period when this itinerary is not worth the journey.

## The paperwork, one country at a time

Two borders, two unrelated schemes, and neither answer exists in the abstract — both belong to your passport. The [Thailand](/en/thailande) and [Laos](/en/laos) pages give the rule for yours, each with the government source it came from and the month it was read. Check them four weeks before departure, not at the bridge.
