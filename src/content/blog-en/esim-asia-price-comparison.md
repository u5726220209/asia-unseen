A month of data in Asia costs between 13,50 € and 68,90 € from the same provider, for exactly the same coverage. The gap has nothing to do with network quality or the number of countries served: it comes down to choosing between a data allowance and an unlimited plan.

That is the only decision that really matters, and most travellers take it the wrong way round.

**A note on currency.** Prices are read from the providers' own pages in euros. Convert at the rate of the day; the euro figures are what was published, not an estimate.

## Who genuinely needs unlimited

Three uses justify it, and only three:

- **Tethering**, if you are feeding a laptop or your travelling companions' phones.
- **Remote work**, with daily video calls.
- **Video on the move**, long bus journeys included.

Outside those cases, 10 GB over a month is ample: browsing, messaging, offline maps topped up occasionally, a few video calls. The real consumption of a traveller who uses their accommodation's wifi sits between 5 and 12 GB a month.

One thing to know about [Holafly](https://esim.holafly.com): the unlimited is real for your phone, but **tethering is capped at 1 GB a day**. If you are buying unlimited precisely to feed a laptop, that limit changes the sum.

## Country eSIM or regional eSIM?

The answer depends on the type of plan, and it is not intuitive.

| Plan, 30 days | Vietnam eSIM | Asia eSIM (18 countries) | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 GB | 16,00 € | 22,50 € | +41 % |
| Unlimited | 61,50 € | 65,50 € | **+6,5 %** |

**On a data-allowance plan, the [country eSIM](https://www.airalo.com) is clearly cheaper.** Buy it if you are visiting one country.

**On an unlimited plan, the gap becomes negligible.** Six and a half euros more to cover seventeen extra countries: on any route that crosses a border, the question does not arise.

## The local SIM, which remains unbeatable

It earns this site nothing, and it is still the best deal on pure price. The AIS tourist SIM in Thailand gives a month of unlimited full-speed data for 1 199 THB, about 31 € — less than half an equivalent unlimited eSIM.

What the eSIM buys in exchange:

- **A connection the moment you land**, before the immigration queue.
- **Your home number kept active**, for banking verification codes — which becomes critical the moment a payment needs confirming.
- **No counter to find and no passport to hand over.** Airport stands sell above the price of city shops, sometimes at double.
- **One installation for the whole route**, instead of a card per border.

Two constraints on the local SIM: it requires a passport to register, and in Thailand the NBTC regulations impose a fresh identity check beyond 60 days of use.

**The honest sum:** for a month in a single country, the local SIM saves about thirty euros. For three weeks across three countries, the regional eSIM wins in time what it loses in euros.

## The Chinese case, which is not about coverage

Airalo's regional eSIM does cover China, via China Unicom, China Mobile and China Telecom. The problem is elsewhere: **an eSIM attached to a Chinese operator routes your traffic through China**, and therefore through the filtering. Google, Gmail, WhatsApp and Instagram stay unreachable, exactly as with a SIM bought locally.

Only an eSIM whose traffic exits via Hong Kong gets around that filtering. Good providers state this; if it is not stated, assume the routing is local.

In every case, install and **test** a VPN from home before you leave: the app stores are filtered too once you are there. [The full China preparation sequence](/en/blog/arriving-in-china-preparation) covers it, alongside payment and train booking.

## What not to do

**Buy unlimited as a precaution.** It is the most expensive reflex of the lot: forty-three euros more a month for a volume most travellers never reach. Start with 10 GB; top-ups take two minutes in the app.

**Install the eSIM after you arrive.** Activation needs an internet connection. With no airport wifi you are stuck with an eSIM you have bought and cannot use. Install it before departure, activate it on landing.

**Rely on unlimited for tethering without checking the tethering cap.** With Holafly it is 1 GB a day.

**Take an eSIM to China without checking the routing.** The plan will work perfectly, and you still will not have Google.
