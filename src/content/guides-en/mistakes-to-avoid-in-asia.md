This list comes from the messages we receive and from the mistakes we have made ourselves. It is ordered by real cost, heaviest first.

## What costs the most money

**1. Hiring a two-wheeler without a valid licence.** Without a motorcycle licence and an international driving permit, your insurance covers nothing: not medical costs, not repatriation — 20,000 to 80,000 € — not third-party damage. By a long way the most expensive mistake available in Southeast Asia, and the most common.

**2. Travelling uninsured on the assumption that a bank card is enough.** Premium card cover often caps medical costs low, over a maximum trip length, and only if the trip was paid for with that card. Read the cap and the length before relying on it.

**3. Going through an intermediary for a visa.** 60 to 120 € for an official formality costing 25 to 36 USD, on a form you will fill in yourself in twenty minutes. Each country page links the government portal directly.

**4. Booking an internal flight with under three hours of connection.** In the Philippines and Indonesia, a delay makes you miss the next leg, and two separate tickets are protected by nobody. Cost: a replacement ticket plus a hotel night.

**5. Ignoring passport validity.** Six months beyond your return date, two blank pages. A passport valid for five and a half months means being refused boarding at the departure airport — trip lost, no recourse.

## What costs time

**6. Underestimating distances.** Two hundred kilometres of mountain in Laos or northern Vietnam is seven hours. Hanoi to Saigon by train is 32 h 45. Look at real journey times before building an itinerary, not at the map.

**7. Chaining too many stops.** Every change of town costs between half a day and a full one. A three-week itinerary with nine stops is a transport itinerary, not a trip.

**8. Arriving in China without a VPN installed and tested.** Google, Gmail, WhatsApp and Instagram are unreachable, and the app stores are filtered too. A VPN is downloaded and tested **before** departure.

**9. Arriving in China without Alipay or WeChat Pay linked to a foreign card.** The country has largely abandoned cash. The linking is done before departure. [The full sequence is here](/en/blog/arriving-in-china-preparation).

**10. Not booking Chinese trains in advance.** They go on sale fifteen days ahead and move fast. A Chinese itinerary is built around the tickets available.

## The classic scams, and the defence

**11. The cut-price tuk-tuk tour.** A city tour at a token price that is in fact a round of commission shops. Defence: decide your destination before getting in, and refuse any unplanned stop.

**12. The two-stage count at the exchange counter.** You are shown the bundle, it is taken back, and a shorter one is returned. Defence: count slowly, at the window, and never hand the notes back.

**13. Dynamic currency conversion.** The terminal offers to charge you "in euros": the rate applied is 3 to 6 % worse. Defence: always local currency, without exception.

**14. "The site is closed today."** A stranger outside the entrance tells you the temple is closed and proposes an alternative. The site is open. Defence: go and check at the official ticket window.

**15. The Ha Long cruise sold on the street.** The boat advertised and the boat you board differ regularly. Defence: go through your accommodation or an identifiable operator.

**16. The scooter hire and the invented damage.** On return you are charged for a pre-existing scratch. Defence: film the vehicle from every angle at handover, in front of the person hiring it to you.

**17. The passport as deposit.** Never. A photocopy or a cash advance is enough, and it is what the regulations provide for in most of the countries concerned. Recovering a passport after a dispute can take days.

## Mistakes of conduct

**18. Animal tourism.** Elephant rides, photographs with a tiger, dolphin shows: there is no acceptable version of these activities. A serious sanctuary offers no contact, no riding and no show.

**19. Orphanage tourism.** Documented by UNICEF as an industry that separates children from living families to supply paid visits. It belongs in no defensible itinerary.

**20. Photographing without asking.** The monks' alms round in Luang Prabang, a ceremony, a face. A gesture and a smile are enough to ask. Refusal is rare; not asking, on the other hand, is visible.

## The three reflexes that prevent nearly all of it

**Decide before you get in.** In a taxi, a tuk-tuk, a boat: destination and price agreed beforehand, never during. Ride-hailing apps — Grab, Gojek, DiDi — remove the problem entirely.

**Check at the source.** A price, a timetable, a closure, an entry rule: at the official window, on the government site, not from the person with an interest in you believing them.

**Slow down.** Almost every mistake on this list is a mistake of haste: an itinerary too dense, a booking made in a panic, a decision taken tired on the way out of an airport. One extra spare day in a plan costs less than any of these twenty.
