The question "when should I go to Asia" has no answer, because Asia spans 45 degrees of latitude and three different climate regimes. The useful question is: **where should I go in the month I can actually travel?**

## The three climate regimes worth knowing

**Mainland Southeast Asia** (Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia) runs on a dry season from November to March and a wet season from May to October, with two scorching transition months in April and May. It is the most legible of the three.

**Northeast Asia** (Japan, South Korea, northern China) has four distinct seasons like Europe, shifted by a notch: a sublime spring in April and May, a heavy humid summer, a brilliant autumn in October and November, a cold dry winter.

**Island Asia** (Indonesia, the Philippines, southern Thailand) is the complicated case, because two islands 500 km apart can have opposite seasons. This is where the real trade-offs live.

## The calendar, month by month

**January–February.** The prime window for mainland Southeast Asia: dry, warm without excess, clear skies. It is also high season, so prices follow. Lunar New Year, which falls in late January or in February, brings Vietnam and China to a halt for a week — full transport, closed shops, multiplied prices. Check the date before booking.

**March.** Excellent across Southeast Asia, and the start of the season in Japan. It is probably the best month for a north-to-south Vietnam route, the only moment when all three regions of the country work at once. One caveat: the crop-burning season fills northern Thailand and Laos with dense haze from March to early April.

**April.** A hinge month. Extreme heat in mainland Southeast Asia — 40 °C in Bangkok, hard work on the Angkor temples. But it is also Songkran in Thailand and cherry blossom in Japan, two perfectly good reasons to go anyway. Japan in April is magnificent and packed; book three months ahead.

**May–June.** The year's best compromise for Japan and South Korea: ideal temperatures, greenery, and markedly fewer people than in April. In Southeast Asia the monsoon sets in, but prices fall and the landscapes turn green again. The dry season begins in Indonesia.

**July–August.** The two hardest months to judge. Favour Indonesia (peak dry season), Japan if you go up in altitude or to Hokkaido, and Mongolia. Avoid the Philippines (typhoons), Thailand's Andaman coast, and northern China (heatwave plus peak domestic travel).

**September.** An underrated month. South Korea and China enter their best period, Indonesia is still dry, and mainland Southeast Asia is at its cheapest. It is the best value-for-money month of the year.

**October.** Superb in Northeast Asia — Korean and Japanese autumn is spectacular. Watch out for Chinese Golden Week, 1 to 7 October: 700 million people travel and every Chinese site is saturated. In the Philippines and central Vietnam this is the peak of typhoons and flooding — and [in Hoi An specifically](/en/blog/hoi-an-when-it-rains), a shower and a flood are not the same thing.

**November–December.** The dry season returns to Southeast Asia with landscapes still green from the monsoon that has just ended: visually the finest moment of the year. November is excellent; December gets more expensive as the holidays approach.

## Three trade-offs that come up constantly

**Thailand's two coasts do not share a season.** The Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi, Koh Lanta) is wet from May to October. The Gulf of Thailand (Koh Samui, Koh Tao, Koh Phangan) stays workable until September and takes on water in October and November. This is the single most useful lever for travelling out of season without suffering for it.

**Vietnam does not have one season but three.** The north from October to April, the centre from February to August, the south from December to April. A full north-to-south route really works in March and April. Outside that you choose a region — [ten days in the north](/en/blog/vietnam-in-10-days) is usually the better trip — or accept rain over a third of the itinerary.

**Angkor in the rain beats Angkor in the dry.** Counter-intuitive but clear: from July to October the moats are full, the stone is dark, the light is soft and the groups have thinned to a third. You will take one hour of downpour a day. [It is well worth the trade](/en/blog/angkor-in-the-rainy-season).

## What to check before you fix the dates

- **Lunar New Year** (Vietnam, China, Korea) — a moving date in late January or February. Multiplied prices, saturated transport, widespread closures.
- **Chinese Golden Week** — 1 to 7 October, every year.
- **Ramadan and Eid** in Indonesia and Malaysia — moving dates, heavy domestic travel over Eid.
- **Songkran** in Thailand, mid-April — an exceptional festival, but the country stops for three days.
- **Japan's Golden Week**, late April to early May — the most expensive period of the year in Japan.
