The question arrives in one message out of three: what does it actually cost. Not a comparison site's vague range, but the number you put in a spreadsheet before booking time off.

Here are the amounts observed, category by category, across nine countries.

**A note on currency.** Everything below was recorded in euros, from spending on the ground. Convert at the rate of the day; a figure converted once and left to age is worse than no figure.

## The three budget levels, defined precisely

Numbers mean nothing without definitions. Here is what our three levels cover, per person per day, **excluding the international flight**.

**Shoestring (from 25 €/day).** A dorm or simple guesthouse, food from streets and markets, local buses and economy-class night trains, one or two paid activities a week. The slowest way to travel and often the richest in encounters.

**Comfort (50 to 120 €/day).** A three-star hotel or a good apartment, two restaurant meals a day, fast transport when it saves half a day, guided visits, a few internal flights. This is what most two- to three-week travellers actually spend.

**Premium (100 to 250 €/day).** A four-star or boutique hotel, a private driver on the long legs, organised excursions, careful restaurants. Beyond that you are in a different market.

## The comparison, nine countries

| Country | Shoestring | Comfort | Premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vietnam | 25 € | 55 € | 120 € |
| Laos | 25 € | 50 € | 100 € |
| Cambodia | 28 € | 55 € | 115 € |
| Thailand | 30 € | 65 € | 150 € |
| Indonesia | 30 € | 60 € | 140 € |
| Philippines | 30 € | 60 € | 130 € |
| China | 40 € | 80 € | 170 € |
| South Korea | 55 € | 100 € | 200 € |
| Japan | 65 € | 120 € | 250 € |

Two useful readings of that table.

First, the gap between Vietnam and Japan is a factor of 2.6 on a shoestring, but only 2.2 at premium. The further up you go, the more prices converge: a good hotel costs a good price everywhere.

Second, the flight dominates a short trip. Over ten days in Vietnam in comfort, the flight is more than half the total. Over a month, under a third. **That is the strongest argument for going for longer**: every extra day costs far less than the average.

## Where the money goes

From day-by-day spending records, the split of an on-the-ground budget holds up remarkably well from one country to the next:

- **Accommodation — 38 %.** The most elastic item, and therefore the first lever.
- **Food and drink — 24 %.** Where the gap between shoestring and comfort is widest, because imported alcohol costs the price of three meals everywhere.
- **Local transport — 14 %.** Except in an archipelago, where it doubles.
- **Sights and activities — 16 %.** The big paid sites — Angkor, trekking permits, diving — create the spikes.
- **Other and contingency — 8 %.** Laundry, SIM, pharmacy, and whatever you break.

## The five levers that genuinely move the total

**Travel in low season.** The most powerful lever by far: 20 to 35 % off accommodation and flights, on exactly the same trip. [Our season guide](/en/guides/best-time-to-visit-asia) sets out where the monsoon is a real problem and where it is one afternoon shower.

**Slow down.** Three nights in one place costs less than three nights in three towns: less transport, tapering rates, and the time to find where local people eat.

**Book accommodation in two stages.** The first two or three nights from home, so you arrive without stress. The rest on the ground or a few days out, once you know how long you want to stay.

**Eat where there is a queue.** A street stall with ten people waiting serves a better plate at 2 € than an empty restaurant with a four-language menu at 12 €. True in all nine countries.

**Eliminate bank charges.** Between a card that takes 3 % plus 5 € per withdrawal and one that takes neither, a month of travel with 1,500 € of spending is about 70 € of difference.

## What people forget to budget

Three items go over every time:

1. **Travel insurance** — 30 to 90 € for three weeks. It is not optional.
2. **Internal flights** — above all in the Philippines and Indonesia. Three internal flights is easily 200 € a person.
3. **The last day** — the airport hotel, the taxi, the expensive meal, the presents bought in a panic. Allow 80 to 120 € that exist in no forecast.

Add **15 % of margin** to whatever total you reach. That is not excessive caution: it is the average observed gap between budgeted and spent. [A month in Vietnam, line by line](/en/blog/one-month-in-vietnam-cost) shows where that 15 % actually went.

## And on the ground, how to track it

The tracking that works is the simplest: a note on your phone, one line per expense, category and amount. Five seconds each. After a week you know exactly where the money goes, and you can adjust without cutting at random.

Budget-tracking apps mostly add friction. What matters is writing it down as you pay — not the tool.
